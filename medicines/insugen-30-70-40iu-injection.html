Product overview

What is Insugen 30/70 40IU/ml Injection?

Insugen 30/70 40IU/ml Injection is a prescription medicine by Biocon Ltd containing Human insulin 30% soluble + 70% isophane, 40 IU/ml. It is a same-salt substitute for Mixtard 30/70 40IU/ml Injection and costs 16% less. Human insulin 30/70 is a premixed insulin: 30% fast-acting soluble insulin and 70% intermediate-acting isophane (NPH) insulin. It controls blood sugar at meals and between meals with one injection. Most people inject it twice a day, about 30 minutes before breakfast and dinner. Premixed human insulin is widely used in India because it is affordable, needs only two injections a day and covers both meal-time and background insulin needs.

Is Insugen 30/70 as good as Mixtard 30/70?

Short answer: yes, when the salt, strength and form match.

Yes, when the salt, strength, dosage form and release type are the same. Human Insulin 30/70 is off patent, so many licensed Indian companies make it. Insugen 30/70 and Mixtard 30/70 are both made under the Drugs and Cosmetics Act, 1940, by licensed manufacturers, and must pass the same Indian Pharmacopoeia tests for strength, purity and how fast the tablet dissolves.

Always the same

  • Active ingredient (salt) and its amount
  • Dosage form and route (how you take it)
  • Release type (normal, SR, modified)
  • Quality standard: Indian Pharmacopoeia
  • Manufacturing rules: Schedule M (GMP)

What can differ

  • Brand name and maker
  • Inactive ingredients such as colour or filler
  • Look of the tablet and pack
  • Price

What Indian rules say

  • Licensing: every medicine maker needs a manufacturing licence under the Drugs and Cosmetics Rules, 1945, and its products are tested against Indian Pharmacopoeia monographs.
  • Prescribing: the Indian Medical Council (Professional Conduct, Etiquette and Ethics) Regulations, 2002 ask doctors to prescribe drugs by generic name as far as possible.
  • Pricing: the National Pharmaceutical Pricing Authority (NPPA) sets ceiling prices for medicines on the national essential list, but brands can still be priced very differently below or outside those caps.

Directions

How to take Insugen 30/70

How to take Insugen 30/70

  1. Wash your hands.
  2. Roll the vial gently between your palms until the insulin is evenly cloudy.
  3. Clean the top, draw air equal to your dose into a 40 IU/ml syringe, inject the air into the vial, then draw your dose.
  4. Inject under the skin of your abdomen or thigh about 30 minutes before a meal, and rotate the site each time.

How it works

The soluble (clear) part starts working in about 30 minutes and covers the meal. The isophane (cloudy) part works more slowly, peaks in 4–12 hours and lasts up to 24 hours, covering the gaps between meals. Mixing them in a fixed 30:70 ratio means fewer injections.

Side effects

Side effects of Insugen 30/70 40IU/ml Injection

Common side effects

  • Low blood sugar
  • Redness, swelling or itching at the injection site
  • Weight gain
  • Lumps under the skin if the same spot is used repeatedly

Usually mild. They often settle as your body adjusts.

Stop and get medical help if you notice

  • Severe low blood sugar: confusion, fits or unconsciousness
  • Low potassium: muscle weakness, irregular heartbeat
  • Widespread rash or breathing difficulty (allergy)

How to manage common side effects

Low blood sugarTake 15 g of fast sugar, recheck in 15 minutes and repeat if still low, then eat a snack. Tell your doctor about frequent lows.
Injection-site lumpsRotate sites every time and do not inject into lumpy areas.
Weight gainSpread carbohydrates evenly through the day and stay active.

Precautions

Warnings and who should not take Insugen 30/70

Warnings and precautions

  • Never change your dose or insulin type without your doctor.
  • Check your sugar more often when you are ill, travelling, exercising more or changing brands.
  • Always match the syringe to the vial strength (40 IU/ml).
  • Teach family members how to recognise and treat low sugar.

Who should not take Insugen 30/70

  • Your blood sugar is low (hypoglycaemia) at the time of the dose
  • You are allergic to human insulin or any ingredient
Good to know

Tips, lifestyle and storage

Tips for taking it

  • Roll the vial gently between your palms until evenly cloudy; do not shake hard.
  • Never use a 100 IU/ml syringe with a 40 IU/ml vial: the dose will be wrong.
  • Rotate injection sites across the abdomen and thighs.
  • Keep a log of your readings to share with your doctor.
  • When switching brands, check your sugar more often for the first 2 weeks.

Lifestyle advice

  • Carry glucose tablets and a diabetes ID card.
  • Store spare insulin in the fridge, never in the freezer.
  • Exercise regularly but check your sugar before and after.
  • Get your eyes, feet and kidneys checked every year.

About Insugen 30/70 and Mixtard 30/70 6

Is Insugen 30/70 a good substitute for Mixtard 30/70?

Both are premixed human insulin 30/70 at 40 IU/ml. Insugen is made by Biocon. A Tablow doctor calls you to guide the switch. Check your sugar more often for 2 weeks.

What is Insugen 30/70 used for?

It controls blood sugar in people with diabetes who need insulin.

Who makes Insugen?

Insugen is made by Biocon Ltd, a Bengaluru-based biopharmaceutical company and a major insulin producer.

Which syringe should I use with Insugen 30/70 40IU?

Use a 40 IU/ml insulin syringe only. A 100 IU/ml syringe will give the wrong dose.

How should Insugen be stored?

Unopened vials in the fridge (2–8°C), never frozen. The vial in use can be kept below 25°C for up to 6 weeks.

Will my insulin dose change when I switch?

Usually the unit dose stays the same, but your doctor may adjust it based on your sugar readings after the switch.

Using Human Insulin 30/70 safely 8

What does 30/70 mean?

30% of the insulin is fast-acting (soluble) and 70% is intermediate-acting (isophane/NPH).

What is the difference between 40 IU and 100 IU insulin?

It is the concentration: 40 or 100 units per ml. You must use the matching syringe or pen, or the dose will be wrong.

How long can an open vial be used?

Up to 6 weeks below 25°C. Write the opening date on the vial.

Can I inject through clothing?

It is not recommended. Inject into clean skin.

Why is my insulin cloudy?

Premixed insulin is meant to be cloudy. Roll it gently before each dose so it is evenly mixed.

Can I travel with insulin?

Yes. Carry it in your hand luggage in a cool pouch, never in checked baggage.

Is human insulin the same as analogue insulin?

No. Analogue insulins are modified to act faster or longer. Human insulin is cheaper but needs injecting 30 minutes before meals.

What should I do if I am sick and cannot eat?

Do not stop insulin without advice. Check your sugar more often and call your doctor for a sick-day plan.
