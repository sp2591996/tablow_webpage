Product overview

What is Glimisave M1 Tablet SR?

Glimisave M1 Tablet SR is a prescription medicine by Eris Lifesciences Ltd containing Glimepiride 1 mg + Metformin 500 mg. It is a same-salt substitute for Glycomet GP 1 Tablet SR and costs 20% less. Glimepiride + metformin is a two-medicine tablet for type 2 diabetes. Glimepiride helps the pancreas release more insulin, and metformin reduces the sugar made by the liver and helps the body use insulin better. Doctors prescribe it when diet, exercise or one medicine alone no longer keeps blood sugar in range. It is one of the most commonly prescribed diabetes combinations in India. Combining two medicines in one tablet means fewer pills, which helps people take their medicine regularly.

What Indian rules say

  • Licensing: every medicine maker needs a manufacturing licence under the Drugs and Cosmetics Rules, 1945, and its products are tested against Indian Pharmacopoeia monographs.

  • Pricing: the National Pharmaceutical Pricing Authority (NPPA) sets ceiling prices for medicines on the national essential list, but brands can still be priced very differently below or outside those caps.

Directions

How to take Glimisave M1

How to take Glimisave M1

  1. Take it with breakfast or the first main meal of the day, unless your doctor gives a different schedule.
  2. Swallow the tablet whole.
  3. do not crush or chew the sustained-release tablet.
  4. Never skip the meal after taking a dose.

How it works

The two medicines tackle different causes of high blood sugar. Glimepiride acts on the pancreas to release more insulin. Metformin lowers the glucose the liver releases and makes muscle and fat cells more sensitive to insulin. The SR (sustained-release) form releases metformin slowly, which is gentler on the stomach.

Side effects

Side effects of Glimisave M1 Tablet SR

Common side effects

  • Low blood sugar: sweating, shaking, hunger, fast heartbeat
  • Nausea or stomach upset
  • Diarrhoea
  • Metallic taste
  • Lower vitamin B12 with long-term use

Usually mild. They often settle as your body adjusts.

Stop and get medical help if you notice

  • Severe low blood sugar: confusion, fainting or fits
  • Lactic acidosis (rare): muscle pain, fast breathing, severe tiredness, vomiting

How to manage common side effects

Low blood sugarEat or drink 15 g of fast sugar (3 glucose tablets, half a glass of juice or 3 teaspoons of sugar in water), recheck after 15 minutes, then have a snack.
Nausea or stomach upsetTake it with food. It usually settles within a few weeks.
Metallic tasteThis is harmless and often fades. Sugar-free mints can help.
Safety advice

Glimisave M1 safety advice

AlcoholUnsafe

Alcohol raises the risk of low blood sugar and of lactic acidosis. Avoid it.

PregnancyUnsafe

Not recommended. Insulin is usually preferred in pregnancy.

BreastfeedingUnsafe

Glimepiride may pass into milk and lower the baby's blood sugar.

DrivingCaution

Low blood sugar can affect concentration. Check your sugar before long drives.

KidneyCaution

Avoid when eGFR is below 30. Kidney function should be checked at least yearly.

LiverCaution

Avoid in severe liver disease.

With food and drink

  • Eat regular meals; do not skip meals after a dose.
  • Limit sugary drinks, sweets and refined carbohydrates.
  • Avoid alcohol: it raises the risk of low sugar and lactic acidosis.

With health conditions

  • Kidney disease: Metformin builds up when the kidneys are weak. It is not used when eGFR is below 30.
  • Liver disease: Raises the risk of low sugar and lactic acidosis. Avoid in severe liver disease.
  • Heart failure or severe infection: Conditions that lower oxygen levels raise the risk of lactic acidosis.
Precautions

Warnings and who should not take Glimisave M1

Warnings and precautions

  • Low blood sugar is more likely in older adults, after exercise, with missed meals or with alcohol.
  • Stop and seek help for symptoms of lactic acidosis: muscle pain, fast breathing, severe tiredness.
  • Pause the medicine before surgery or a CT scan with contrast, as your doctor advises.
  • Long-term use can lower vitamin B12; numbness or tingling should be checked.

Who should not take Glimisave M1

  • You have type 1 diabetes or diabetic ketoacidosis
  • You have severe kidney disease (eGFR below 30) or severe liver disease
  • You are allergic to sulfonamides or sulfonylureas
  • You are pregnant or breastfeeding
  • You drink heavily, or have a condition that lowers oxygen levels (acute heart failure, shock)
Good to know

Tips, lifestyle and storage

Tips for taking it

  • Take it with a meal and never skip the meal after a dose.
  • Carry glucose tablets or sweets for low-sugar episodes.
  • Check HbA1c every 3 months and vitamin B12 once a year.
  • Tell your doctor before any surgery or CT scan with contrast.
  • Walk for 30 minutes a day; it improves how well the medicine works.

Lifestyle advice

  • Walk 30 minutes a day, five days a week.
  • Choose whole grains, vegetables, pulses and lean protein.
  • Check your feet daily for cuts or sores.
  • Keep a log of your sugar readings to share with your doctor.
StorageStore below 30°C, away from moisture. Keep out of reach of children.

About Glimisave M1 and Glycomet GP 1 6

Is Glimisave M1 a good substitute for Glycomet GP 1?

Yes. Both contain glimepiride 1 mg + metformin 500 mg SR. Glimisave M1 is made by Eris Lifesciences. Check your sugar a little more often for the first two weeks after any switch.

What is Glimisave M1 used for?

It controls blood sugar in adults with type 2 diabetes, alongside diet and exercise.

When should I take Glimisave M1?

With breakfast or your first main meal. Swallow it whole; do not crush the SR tablet.

Who makes Glimisave M1?

Glimisave M1 is made by Eris Lifesciences Ltd, an Ahmedabad-based company focused on chronic diseases.

Can Glimisave M1 cause weight gain?

Glimepiride can cause mild weight gain; metformin tends to be weight-neutral. Diet and activity matter most.

Using Glimepiride + Metformin safely 8

Can glimepiride + metformin cause weight gain?

Glimepiride can cause mild weight gain. Metformin tends to be weight-neutral.

What is the best time to take it?

With breakfast or the first main meal of the day, unless your doctor says otherwise.

Can I take it if I am fasting?

Fasting raises the risk of low sugar. Speak to your doctor before any fast, including religious fasts.

Does it damage the kidneys?

No, but metformin must be stopped if kidney function falls a lot, so kidney tests are needed at least once a year.

What does SR mean?

Sustained release: the metformin is released slowly, which is gentler on the stomach and allows once-daily dosing.

Can I stop it once my sugar is normal?

No. Normal readings usually mean the medicine is working. Stopping can make sugar rise again.

Is it safe with blood pressure medicines?

Generally yes, but beta blockers can hide low-sugar symptoms. Tell your doctor about all your medicines.

Why do I need a B12 test?

Long-term metformin can lower vitamin B12, which can cause tiredness and nerve tingling.
