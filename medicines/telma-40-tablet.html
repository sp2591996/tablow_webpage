About Telma 40 Tablet

Uses, dosage, side effects and safety. This applies to Telmikind 40 too, as both contain Telmisartan 40 mg.

Overview

What is Telma 40 Tablet?

Telma 40 Tablet is a prescription medicine made by Glenmark Pharmaceuticals Ltd that contains Telmisartan 40 mg. Telmisartan is a blood pressure medicine from the angiotensin receptor blocker (ARB) group. It relaxes blood vessels so blood flows more easily, which lowers blood pressure and eases the strain on the heart. It is taken once a day and is also used to lower the risk of heart attack and stroke in people at high risk. Telmisartan is widely used because one daily tablet controls blood pressure for 24 hours and it rarely causes the dry cough seen with ACE inhibitors.

Directions

How to take Telma 40

How to take Telma 40

  1. Take one tablet once a day, at the same time each day.
  2. Swallow it whole with water, with or without food.
  3. Keep the tablet in its strip until you take it.

How it works

Angiotensin II is a hormone that narrows blood vessels and makes the body hold on to salt and water. Telmisartan blocks the receptors this hormone acts on, so the blood vessels relax and widen and blood pressure falls. Its long action means one tablet covers a full day. Telmikind 40 contains the same salt, so it works in exactly the same way.

Side effects

Side effects of Telma 40 Tablet

Common side effects

  • Dizziness, especially when standing up
  • Back pain
  • Diarrhoea
  • Sinus or throat infection
  • Tiredness

Usually mild. They often settle as your body adjusts.

Stop and get medical help if you notice

  • Swelling of the face, lips, tongue or throat (angioedema)
  • Fainting or very low blood pressure
  • Muscle weakness or irregular heartbeat (high potassium)
  • Passing much less urine than usual (kidney problems)

How to manage common side effects

DizzinessStand up slowly, sit or lie down if dizzy, and drink enough water.
Back or muscle painUsually mild. Tell your doctor if it persists or comes with weakness.
DiarrhoeaDrink fluids. Tell your doctor if it is severe, as dehydration can drop blood pressure further.
Safety advice

Is Telma 40 safe with alcohol, in pregnancy and while driving?

AlcoholCaution

Alcohol can lower blood pressure further and cause dizziness. Limit it.

PregnancyUnsafe

Can harm the unborn baby. Stop and see your doctor as soon as you know you are pregnant.

BreastfeedingConsult your doctor

Not recommended; other medicines are usually preferred.

DrivingCaution

Can cause dizziness, especially at the start. Do not drive if affected.

KidneyCaution

Kidney function and potassium should be checked. No dose change is usually needed in mild disease.

LiverCaution

Lower doses in mild to moderate liver disease; avoid in severe disease.

What interacts with Telma 40?

With food and drink

  • Can be taken with or without food.
  • Keep salt under 5 g a day (about one teaspoon).
  • Avoid potassium-based salt substitutes unless your doctor approves.
  • Limit alcohol.

With health conditions

  • Kidney disease: Kidney function and potassium need regular checks.
  • Liver disease: Lower doses; avoid with blocked bile ducts or severe liver disease.
  • Narrowed kidney arteries (renal artery stenosis): Can cause a sudden drop in kidney function.
Precautions

Warnings and who should not take Telma 40

Warnings and precautions

  • Do not take it during pregnancy; tell your doctor if you plan to conceive.
  • Vomiting, diarrhoea or heavy sweating can cause low blood pressure; ask your doctor whether to pause it.
  • Kidney function and potassium may be checked 1–2 weeks after starting or changing the dose.
  • Tell the surgeon or anaesthetist you take it before any operation.

Who should not take Telma 40

  • You are pregnant or planning a pregnancy
  • You have severe liver disease or blocked bile ducts
  • You have diabetes or kidney disease and take aliskiren
  • You are allergic to telmisartan
Good to know

Tips, lifestyle and storage for Telma 40

Tips for taking it

  • Take it at the same time every day, with or without food.
  • Stand up slowly to avoid dizziness.
  • Keep taking it even when you feel well; high blood pressure usually has no symptoms.
  • Check your blood pressure at home and keep a record.
  • Do not use potassium-based salt substitutes without advice.

Lifestyle advice

  • Cut down on salt, pickles, papad and packaged snacks.
  • Aim for 150 minutes of brisk walking a week.
  • Keep a healthy weight and waist size.
  • Stop smoking and limit alcohol.
  • Check your blood pressure at home and share the log with your doctor.
StorageStore below 30°C. Keep tablets in the strip until use, as they absorb moisture. Keep out of reach of children.

Using Telmisartan safely 8

What is the best time to take telmisartan?

Any time of day, as long as it is the same time daily. Many people take it in the morning.

Can I stop telmisartan once my blood pressure is normal?

No. Normal readings usually mean the medicine is working. Stopping can make blood pressure rise again.

Does telmisartan cause cough?

Rarely. Dry cough is much more common with ACE inhibitors such as ramipril.

Is telmisartan safe for the kidneys?

It can protect the kidneys in diabetes and high blood pressure, but kidney function and potassium should be checked regularly.

How long does telmisartan take to work?

Blood pressure starts falling within hours, but the full effect takes about 4 weeks.

What is the difference between telmisartan 40 mg and 80 mg?

80 mg is twice the dose and lowers blood pressure a little more. Your doctor chooses the strength from your readings.

Can I take telmisartan with amlodipine?

Yes. They are often combined, and combination tablets are available.

Is telmisartan safe in pregnancy?

No. It can harm the unborn baby. Speak to your doctor before planning a pregnancy.
