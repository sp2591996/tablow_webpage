Foundation

1. Commerce model

1Branded page = traffic page

People search the brand on their prescription (“Augmentin 625”). This page ranks for the brand, explains it fully, and has no add-to-cart for the brand. Every buying action adds the substitute instead.

2Substitute page = buy page

The only product sold. It has the full e-commerce kit: price, pack choice, quantity, add to cart, pincode check and trust signals, plus a full medicine monograph.

This is the PlatinumRx model, which sells only the substitute and uses brand pages to direct traffic, rather than the Truemeds model, which sells both. It keeps Tablow's promise simple: same salt, same effect, lower bills. Salt and company pages are hubs that connect both page types.

Where Tablow should win

  • One clear pick, not a list. Recommending one substitute (the Smart Switch) removes choice overload; other brands appear only on the salt page.
  • Savings always measured against the brand, in both ₹ and %, per unit and per pack, plus monthly and yearly savings in a calculator.
  • Proof before price: a seven-row exact-match table (salt, strength, form, release type, pack, Rx, quality standard).
  • Explain why it is cheaper, so people don't assume a lower price means a weaker medicine.
  • Company pages with real content, where 1mg has only link lists.
Rule

Top of the page is worth the most

Most people never scroll far. The value of space falls as you go down, so the top must answer the decision; detail goes lower.

ZoneWhat goes thereWhat never goes there
First screen (top ~20–30%)Why switch, that it is the same medicine, how much you save, the price and the add button. Safety caveats (insulin, levothyroxine) when they apply.Marketing banners, long intros, author lines, company facts, brand details, decorative images, more than one tagline.
Next screenThree short answers (same? quality? saving?), the 30-second summary.Tables and long lists.
BelowDetailed comparison, calculator, generic vs brand, the full monograph, FAQs, references.—
  • Budget on phones (375×812): site chrome (banner, header, search, breadcrumb) at most ~130px; the saving and the add button on the first screen on every medicine page.
  • Phones drop low-value items from the top: the top orange bar, all but the last two breadcrumb levels, brand pills and strength chips on the brand panel, attribute tiles, highlight lists and stat tiles (their facts stay in the overview lists lower down).
  • Calm type: weight 700 only for the page title, prices and the saving figure; 600 for headings and emphasis; body 400–500. No more than one large statement per card.
  • Add to cart never blocks, and copy never adds friction: no “prescription needed”, “upload first” or “check with your doctor before switching” near the buy button. Prescription medicines say “No prescription? A Tablow doctor calls you after you order.” Safety notes stay only where clinically needed (insulin, levothyroxine) and are framed as the Tablow doctor guiding the switch.
  • One button in the hero: Add to cart. Details sit in a small “Good to switch · same salt, strength and form · Details” line, not a second large button.
  • Savings are always per year: hero, summary, price table, buy boxes and calculator use one basis (daily dose × 365, vials × 12, or 3 courses a year for short courses, whole packs). The calculator asks one question and answers in ₹ per year.

Example: Abbott India (it makes a brand and a substitute). Header card with monogram, ticker, about line and six stat tiles (founded, HQ, listing, founder, parent, medicines on Tablow). Check founder, ticker and parent facts against the company's annual report before publishing.

Conversion

7. Building trust and showing affordability

Do

  • Always compare against the brand, never only against MRP.
  • Show ₹ saved and % saved together, plus per-unit prices.
  • Put proof (match table, maker, quality standard) next to the price.
  • Name the maker on every card. Established names build trust.
  • Give the pharmacist a visible role: verified match, WhatsApp help.
  • Use honest copy: “Ask your doctor”, “same salt, strength and form”.
  • Once real data exists, add social proof such as “X people switched this month”.

Don't

  • Claim a substitute is “better” or “more effective”.
  • Use fake reviews, ratings or counters. Add them only when they are real.
  • Call two products a match when the release type (SR vs IR) or strength differs.
  • Show coupons or cashback on Schedule H/H1 medicines.
  • Hide the brand price. Transparency is the trust signal.

Structured data

9. JSON-LD per page (one @graph)

  • Branded: MedicalWebPage (lastReviewed, about → Drug, mentions → substitute Product) + Drug (brand, isProprietary, warnings, storage) + Product/Offer for the substitute (the only thing on sale) + BreadcrumbList + FAQPage + Organization. There is deliberately no Offer for the brand, because it is not sold.
  • Substitute: MedicalWebPage + Drug + Product (Offer in INR, availability, shipping to IN, isSimilarTo → brand) + BreadcrumbList + FAQPage.
  • Salt: MedicalWebPage + Drug (availableStrength) + ItemList of brands + BreadcrumbList + FAQPage.
  • Company: CollectionPage + Organization (address, foundingDate, knowsAbout) + ItemList + BreadcrumbList + FAQPage.
  • Google retired FAQ rich results in May 2026. FAQPage stays only as semantic markup for AI engines. Validate everything in the Rich Results Test and the Schema.org validator.
Writing

10. Content style

  • Plain English at about a Class 8 reading level. Short sentences, one idea per paragraph.
  • Lead with the answer, then the detail. Each “What is” block is 40–60 words.
  • Use tables for comparisons, doses and prices, and lists for side effects and tips.
  • Use the exact brand and salt names consistently. Don't stuff keywords.
  • Clinical claims must match the prescribing information, with no efficacy superlatives.
  • Every page shows its author, a named reviewer, the last-updated date, references and a disclaimer.

Go-live

13. Launch checklist

  • Wire live catalogue prices (MRP and any Tablow price) into both the page and the JSON-LD.
  • Confirm each substitute's salt, strength and release type against the pack. Fill in the reviewing pharmacist's name and registration.
  • Replace the pack illustrations with real photos (WebP, front/back/label, with alt text).
  • Connect add to cart, pincode serviceability and prescription upload to the real backend.
  • Serve the pages as SSR HTML at the canonical URLs. Submit seo/sitemap-medicines.xml and publish llms.txt.
  • Run Lighthouse (aim for 90+ on SEO, accessibility and performance) and the Rich Results Test. Test at 360 px.
  • Measure: brand-page → substitute add-to-cart rate, organic clicks per pair, and AI citations for “substitute for [brand]”.
