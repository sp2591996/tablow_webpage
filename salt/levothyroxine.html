Salt overview

What is Levothyroxine?

Levothyroxine is a man-made version of thyroxine (T4), the main hormone made by the thyroid gland. It treats an underactive thyroid (hypothyroidism) by replacing the hormone your body is missing. Most people take it once a day on an empty stomach, usually for life, with the dose set by a TSH blood test. Hypothyroidism is common in India, especially in women. Because the right dose is very precise, levothyroxine comes in many small strength steps.

Indications

What is Levothyroxine used for?

HypothyroidismReplaces missing thyroid hormone and relieves tiredness, weight gain, feeling cold and low mood.
After thyroid surgery or radioactive iodineReplaces the hormone when the thyroid has been removed or treated.
Goitre and thyroid nodulesSometimes used to shrink an enlarged thyroid.
Hypothyroidism in pregnancyKeeps the mother's levels normal, which matters for the baby's brain development.
Directions

How to take Levothyroxine

How to take Levothyroxine

  1. Take the tablet in the morning on an empty stomach with a glass of water.
  2. Wait 30–60 minutes before breakfast, tea or coffee.
  3. Take it at the same time every day.
  4. Keep calcium, iron and antacids at least 4 hours apart.

How it works

Levothyroxine is identical to the T4 hormone your thyroid normally releases. The body converts it into the active hormone T3, which controls metabolism: how fast your cells use energy. The right daily dose brings hormone levels back to normal over 4–6 weeks.

Typical doses for Levothyroxine. Always follow the dose on your prescription.

If you miss a doseTake it as soon as you remember the same day. If it is nearly time for the next dose, skip the missed one. Do not double up; missing an occasional dose is not dangerous.
If you take too muchToo much can cause a fast heartbeat, chest pain, shaking, sweating and anxiety. Seek medical help, especially if you have heart disease.
Side effects

Side effects of Levothyroxine

Common side effects

  • Usually none at the right dose
  • Palpitations or fast heartbeat (dose too high)
  • Feeling hot, sweating, anxiety or poor sleep (dose too high)
  • Temporary hair thinning in the first months
  • Tiredness and weight gain (dose too low)

Usually mild. They often settle as your body adjusts.

Stop and get medical help if you notice

  • Chest pain or an irregular, very fast heartbeat
  • Severe headache, shaking or fits
  • Swelling of the face or throat (allergy, very rare)

How to manage common side effects

Palpitations or feeling jitteryYour dose may be too high. Tell your doctor; a TSH test can check.
Trouble sleepingTake it early in the morning and limit caffeine. Report it if it continues.
Hair thinningUsually temporary in the first few months and settles as levels stabilise.
Safety advice

Levothyroxine safety advice

AlcoholSafe

No known interaction. Moderate drinking does not affect it.

PregnancySafe if prescribed

Safe and essential if you have hypothyroidism. The dose usually needs to go up, so check TSH early.

BreastfeedingSafe

Safe. Only normal hormone levels pass into milk.

DrivingSafe

Does not affect driving.

KidneySafe

No dose change is usually needed.

LiverSafe

No dose change is usually needed.

Interactions

Interactions of Levothyroxine

With other medicines

Tell your doctor about every medicine, supplement and herbal product you take.

MedicineSeverityWhat can happen
Calcium, iron, antacids, multivitaminsMajorBlock absorption. Take them at least 4 hours apart.
Pantoprazole, omeprazole and other PPIsModerateCan reduce absorption over time; TSH may need checking.
WarfarinModerateCan increase warfarin's effect; INR needs monitoring when the dose changes.
Diabetes medicines and insulinModerateBlood sugar may rise as thyroid levels improve.
Soy, high-fibre food, coffeeMinorReduce absorption if taken at the same time.

With food and drink

  • Take it on an empty stomach, 30–60 minutes before food.
  • Keep milk, curd and calcium-rich food away from the dose.
  • Tea and coffee reduce absorption; wait at least 30 minutes.
  • Use iodised salt as normal; avoid iodine or kelp supplements unless advised.

With health conditions

  • Heart disease: Start with a low dose and increase slowly, as too much can strain the heart.
  • Diabetes: Sugar control may change as thyroid levels improve; monitor your sugar.
  • Adrenal insufficiency: Must be treated before starting levothyroxine.
Precautions

Warnings and who should not take it

Warnings and precautions

  • Levothyroxine has a narrow dosing range: small changes in dose or brand can change your TSH.
  • Never use it for weight loss; high doses can cause serious heart problems.
  • Tell your doctor if you become pregnant, as the dose usually needs to rise.
  • Older adults are more sensitive to over-treatment, which can affect the heart and bones.

Who should not take Levothyroxine

  • You have an untreated overactive thyroid (thyrotoxicosis)
  • You have untreated adrenal insufficiency
  • You have had a recent heart attack, unless your doctor is managing it
  • You want to use it for weight loss: this is dangerous
Good to know

Tips, lifestyle and storage

Tips for taking it

  • Take it on an empty stomach, 30–60 minutes before breakfast, with water only.
  • Keep calcium, iron and antacids at least 4 hours away.
  • Stay on one brand, and recheck TSH 6–8 weeks after any brand or dose change.
  • Do not stop it when you feel better; most people need it for life.
  • Tell your doctor straight away if you become pregnant.

Lifestyle advice

  • Set a daily alarm so you take it at the same time every morning.
  • Eat a balanced diet; cabbage and cauliflower in normal amounts are fine.
  • Exercise regularly to help energy and weight.
  • Keep a record of your TSH results and doses.
StorageStore below 25°C in the original bottle, tightly closed, away from light and moisture. Keep out of reach of children.

Using it safely 8

Why must levothyroxine be taken on an empty stomach?

Food, tea, coffee, milk and many supplements reduce how much is absorbed. An empty stomach gives steady, predictable levels.

Can I take levothyroxine at night?

Some doctors allow a bedtime dose, at least 3–4 hours after the last meal. Choose one time and stick to it.

How long does levothyroxine take to work?

Many people feel better in 2–3 weeks, but TSH takes 6–8 weeks to settle, which is why tests are done then.

Will I need levothyroxine for life?

Most people with hypothyroidism do. Some temporary causes, such as thyroiditis after pregnancy, need it only for a while.

Does levothyroxine cause weight gain?

Not at the right dose. Untreated hypothyroidism can cause weight gain. Levothyroxine is not a weight-loss medicine.

Can I switch levothyroxine brands?

Yes. A Tablow doctor calls you to guide the switch. Recheck TSH 6–8 weeks after switching, then stay with the same brand.

Is levothyroxine safe in pregnancy?

Yes, and it is important. The dose often needs to go up, so tell your doctor as soon as you are pregnant.

What is the difference between 25, 50 and 100 mcg?

They are dose steps. Your doctor picks the strength from your weight, age and TSH, and may combine tablets for in-between doses.
